# Dense head movement measurement — 2026-10-11

Implemented locally in desktop and mobile recording flows. Not deployed to Hugging Face. This is a descriptive image-motion measurement, separate from the existing three observation-group grades. No effort, muscle tension or clinical tremor grade is inferred from these coordinates.

## Acquisition and analysis

`static/neck-motion.js` seeds six head patches from recent preview face landmarks. It tracks textured 9×9 grayscale patches at image width 256, with subpixel refinement, alongside up to twelve textured border patches. Median head displacement minus median background translation is accumulated in units of image width. At least three head and four background matches are required. Background translation compensation does not remove camera rotation, parallax, rolling shutter or articulation. Points lost during the recording are not automatically reacquired.

The browser uses `requestVideoFrameCallback` and media timestamps, capped at roughly 30 samples/second. It runs alongside the existing 5fps image-upload path and does not send additional images to OpenAI. Only bounded derived samples are posted with the same WAV and current neck session to `/api/neck-effort/recording/stop`. Payload limits are 128KiB, 900 samples and 25 seconds; timestamps must increase and coordinates must be finite and bounded. A missing or stale face seed, unsupported browser callback or missing textured background can leave the metric unavailable.

`head_motion.py` analyzes two-second windows stepped by one second. Median sampling rate must be at least 12Hz; a window needs at least 20 usable samples, 80% usable coverage, 1.7 seconds of span and no usable-sample gap above 0.12 seconds. Short gaps are interpolated onto a uniform time grid, the trace is linearly detrended and bandpass filtered from 2Hz to min(8Hz, 0.4 × sampling rate). RMS vector displacement is reported as percent image width. A Welch spectral peak describes the strongest frequency in this band. These are exploratory engineering settings, not clinical thresholds.

Missing windows retain null measurements. The filter intentionally excludes slow movement and does not characterize every possible tremor frequency. Small residual noise can still have a spectral peak; frequency must be read with amplitude and tracking quality. Absolute amplitudes cannot be compared across different camera distances as physical movement magnitudes.

The derived report is stored in the existing neck-recording JSON and rendered even when OpenAI returns a structured failure. Raw tracking samples are not stored in the recording summary. Existing group grades are unchanged. Regular users see the motion measurements and concise explanation; sampling, frequency-band and background diagnostics are in the closed administrator panel. Client-side administrator presentation is not a server-side data access restriction. A network failure preventing receipt of the entire stop response can still prevent display of this report.

## Verification

- 60 existing/extended Python neck tests and five new head-motion tests passed. Coverage includes current-session enforcement, invalid-payload rejection, persistence with a failing mock provider, sparse sampling and missing data.
- JavaScript tests passed for capture batching/cancellation, motion tracking, desktop/mobile form submission and result rendering. The original three group grades remain visible and motion remains visible on a simulated provider failure.
- The actual patch tracker was tested on generated image sequences: static image, 4.4Hz head translation, slow 0.5Hz head translation and whole-frame 4.4Hz camera translation. Static amplitude was zero; fast head motion was approximately 0.490–0.508% of image width; slow movement was approximately 0.0017–0.0021%; camera-compensated residual was approximately 0.0023–0.0130%. The test bounds distinguish these controlled cases and are not production diagnostic cutoffs. See `synthetic_traces.json` and `synthetic_reports.json`.
- Actual clip: `data/WIN_20261011_10_33_38_Pro.mp4`, 257 decoded frames over approximately 9.14 seconds. Offline execution of the browser tracker core retained 256 usable samples. Eight windows were measurable; amplitudes were approximately 0.146–0.235% of image width, with peaks approximately 3.07–4.60Hz. See `real_video_trace.json` and `real_video_report.json`. This narrower-window estimate differs from the earlier whole-clip optical-flow peak around 4.4Hz; the methods are not interchangeable.
- Actual Chromium playback and capture on desktop and mobile-sized pages passed. The browser delivered about 15.625 samples/second: desktop 137 samples and four usable windows of eight; mobile 139 samples and five usable windows of eight. Other windows remained unavailable because of timing gaps. Measured amplitudes were approximately 0.122–0.174% on desktop and 0.122–0.196% on mobile; most usable spectral peaks were around 4.54–4.69Hz, with one mobile window at 4.17Hz. No JavaScript errors were observed. Regular-user diagnostics were hidden, group grades were preserved and local motion remained visible on simulated provider failure. See `browser_results.json` and the two browser trace files.

The Chromium check played the existing video locally and supplied first-frame face anchors. It did not test a physical webcam, mobile hardware or a new OpenAI request. API persistence was tested separately with a mock provider. The earlier real-provider clip experiment is documented in `../recording_10_33_38_2026_10_11/REPORT.md`; the new metric makes no provider call and consumes no additional API tokens.

## Remaining scientific work

There is one real clip with user-reported shaking, without independent labels for muscle activity. The controls are synthetic. This establishes feasibility of measuring a visible oscillation, not accuracy of neck effort estimation. Next validation requires real recordings with independent annotations of visible movement, camera/torso movement controls, repeatability across devices and, if estimating muscle activity, an appropriate external reference such as synchronized sEMG. No neural model was trained or calibrated by this change.

For live use, refresh the browser, allow the preview to detect the face, keep some textured stationary background visible and record normally. The metric is generated for new recordings; old reports without dense samples are not retroactively measured.
